Chitwan National Park, Nepal // 27.50 N

Humans and Rhinos: One Coexistence

Words and photos by Nischal Kunwar

A living landscape

In Nepal’s southern Terai, Chitwan National Park is a landscape shaped by forests, grasslands, wetlands, and rivers. It is also home to the greater one-horned rhinoceros, an animal whose presence has become closely tied to the story of conservation in the region.

What does it take for people and wildlife to share a landscape?

The question reaches beyond the park boundary. Wildlife moves through a wider landscape, while nearby communities live, work, and make decisions that shape its future. Coexistence is not a simple promise; it depends on listening to local experience, protecting habitats, and making room for both people and animals.

This story follows that relationship: the wonder of encountering a rhinoceros in its habitat, and the everyday work behind keeping that encounter possible. The details here are a starting draft to build on with your own observations and sources.
